# primes are the information

For a completely multiplicative +/-1 sequence f, every composite value is determined by earlier values: f(n) = f(p) f(n/p). Only the primes carry fresh bits. An observer that knows multiplicativity pays exactly (prime density) x H(f at primes) bits/symbol. E002 measured this: Legendre (n/1000003) costs 0.138 bits/symbol = prime density, and Liouville costs 0.001 (lambda(p) = -1 is learned at once). Generic observers (KT, CTW-16) pay 1.00 on both.

**Source:** E002 section B; Sarnak's Moebius-disjointness conjecture (mu is uncorrelated with every zero-entropy sequence), which is number theory's form of observer-relative pseudorandomness.
