jev / calibrated decisions

Most expensive AI computation — chain-of-thought, search, sampling and voting, simulation, a second model acting as judge — exists to produce one small decision. We study when that decision can be made in a single forward pass by a model that is calibrated: one whose 80% means right four times in five.

A calibrated fast model does two things a fast model alone cannot. It can be trusted at the confidence it states, so its outputs can be fed straight into expected-value decisions. And it knows when to stop trusting itself: low confidence is the signal to spend real compute. Done well, the expensive path is taken only where it changes the answer, and what it finds can be distilled back into the fast path.

Open questions

  1. How much deliberation can be amortized? For which tasks does a one-pass model match the slow procedure, and where does architecture cap it?
  2. What does confidence buy? The fraction of slow-path calls a confidence gate saves at matched quality, measured against random routing and a perfect oracle.
  3. Does the loop learn? Whether distilling escalated cases back in improves the fast model faster than random labelling, and what it does to calibration.
  4. Where does calibration break? Under distribution shift, intuition fails quietly. Can the model notice it has left familiar ground?
  5. Where do labels come from? Calibration needs ground truth. That can come from verifiers, simulators, search, self-play, or outcomes that resolve later.

What this suggests:

  • Proper-score training does not give calibration for free; recalibration on held-out data is necessary.
  • Confidence routing helps, but most of the remaining gap is in resolution: telling right answers from wrong ones. That is a separate problem from calibration.
  • Calibration degrades off-distribution even where accuracy holds.
  • The naive "escalate, then distil back" loop gave no gain over random labelling here. That is a negative result, and it points to the next experiment.

All figures are means over 3 seeds.

Testbeds

  • Exact oracles (graphs, puzzles, arithmetic): unlimited clean labels and a known ceiling.
  • Chess: search is the slow path and game results are the outcomes. Our small bot jess-hyperbullet plays on Lichess. Its confidence is not calibrated yet, which is the point of testing it.
  • Language decisions: multiple choice, yes/no and graded scores, where one pass over option markers replaces sampling and judging.
  • Resolving forecasts: questions that are settled later by the world, which gives a continual and uncheatable source of labels.

Method

Strictly proper scoring rules. Held-out task families. Calibration measured with debiased and smooth estimators, with bootstrap intervals. Several seeds. Negative results reported. Runs default to a laptop, and no cloud compute is spent without a written reason.
